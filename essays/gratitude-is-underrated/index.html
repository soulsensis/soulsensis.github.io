Gratitude is underrated

4 October 2026The opening here, 1 minThe whole essay is in the book

Gratitude is underrated as fuck. Perhaps modern society has rendered it “wishy-washy”, a bit cringe, but on application to your life, you notice insane benefits which go beyond just “positive thinking”. In life, there is no positive and negative, other than how you perceive something to be.

Gratitude is a feeling. It is neither positive nor negative. Feelings are frequential, i.e. gratitude exists in the upper echelons of a higher frequency. But gratitude is rather meaningless to many young people, especially teenagers, or irate customers who have waited in line too long. As gratitude is the last thing on their mind and they would most likely view it negatively. After all, in their respective circumstances, they would say there is nothing to be grateful for.

You see, perception is key. Gratitude is a feeling generated by a synthesis of thoughts which yield the gratitude “fruit” so to speak. When you experience it, you are eating the fruit, the yield of your thinking.

Thinking is attention. You allocate, every day, whether you like it or not, certain thoughts towards certain things. You might think thoughts are random but they do not need to be. Those who think thoughts are random experience exactly that: random thoughts. But those who think more intentionally and pay attention to exactly what they are thinking are able to direct themselves to thinking on aspects of their lives to generate the “fruits” of their labour, the labour of thinking itself. The labour of placing attention on what you are grateful for, no matter the event which occurs.
