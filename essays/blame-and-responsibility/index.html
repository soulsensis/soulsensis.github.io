Blame and responsibility

10 November 20265 min readNarrated on YouTube

Watch it narrated

Subscribe on YouTube

The moment my daughter was born, blame became useless to me, not gradually, not philosophically, but immediately and practically, because I understood something I had not understood before: she would learn from watching me, and every pattern of self-blame, every loop of guilt, every inherited shame I had carried without questioning, she would absorb as the natural way a person moves through the world. And I refused to pass that on. We all have a responsibility to stop intergenerational trauma from destroying our bloodlines. That work begins not with grand declarations but with a single, quiet decision to no longer let borrowed pain run your life.

That decision starts with seeing clearly, and one of the clearest mirrors available to you is other people, because aspects of other people that you do not like are usually aspects of yourself that you do not like, traits you've had during previous life sagas which you are struggling to let go, now enforced as envy, so that when you see the youth drinking and partying, you are reminded of your current life or certain imposed conditions, like having children, which now preclude you from doing that. But the carefree feeling you are mourning was never taken from you by age. You handed it to a social narrative that told you adulthood meant weight without joy. You bought the script. The freedom was always yours to reclaim.

Remember, a key purpose of life is evolution, and if you keep looking backwards with nostalgia and reminiscence, it weakens your ability to be present, and you become a person who keeps looking in the rear view mirror rather than what is right in front of you, and even if the universe brings forth the most beautiful sceneries or opportunities, you will not see and cherish your own reality. We are inundated with content, news, social feeds, previous generations and their remarks, all purporting deficiencies in our character, flooding your nervous system with guilt and shame until your amygdala fires, stress takes over, and you lose the capacity to take personal responsibility entirely. You freeze. And in that freeze, the world happens to you rather than for you. Eliminating these emotions is not weakness. It is the prerequisite for everything that follows.

And yet most people never get past that freeze. They remain locked in the loop, blaming, consuming, numbing, never arriving at the question that changes everything.

What one strives for is unity of heart and mind, and by not constraining oneself to pathways laid by society or societal expectations, something becomes possible that the script never offered: the ability to direct one's own life rather than perform someone else's version of it. There is a point you can arrive at where you no longer blame anyone for anything. And it is the first taste of true liberation. To place blame on an entity causes you to feel weakness, and if you do not blame anyone externally, you wrestle with self-blame, and self-blame is destruction dressed as accountability. The difference between self-blame and responsibility is radical. One dismantles you. The other directs you.

That distinction became undeniable for me the moment I became a father, because the blame did not dissolve gradually, it became simply useless, a false belief I had purchased somewhere along the way, constructed perhaps by the world around me, but maintained entirely by me, and I did not want to fight it anymore. I just wanted to feel content. So I let it go and assumed responsibility for all that occurs in my own life. That is the most powerful way to live as a conscious creator of one's life.

Responsibility to direct your own life, like how a director orchestrates a movie scene, and you adopt the same skillset, because your life is the grandest movie of all, yet many treat themselves as a side character, and in life you are the director and the actor with no separation, and your thoughts carry the energetic potential which crystallises desires into concrete physical being and existence. This is why blaming others is truly pointless. The way you think, the kind of aspects of life you mentally entertain, come into fruition. Like an architectural engineer invoking their creation, you are doing the same with your life.

"But so many things happen to me that are clearly not my fault."

Have you ever stopped to think about your own thinking? Have you started to see events of your life happening for you rather than to you? Perspective is one hell of a drug. It can neutralise absolutely any negativity so that you can move smoothly forward in life, rather than blame the world and cause your own victimhood. It has become so easy to perpetually blame the world for misfortunes to such an extent that even if beauty showed up, you would be blind to it.

I will say this honestly. Life is actually really good. Enjoyable, fun. I just do not see it well enough and that is on me. Behind all the perceived negativity, I am starting to see all the beauty. That admission is not defeat. It is the beginning of direction. Things will be alright as long as I am alright in my mind and heart, as long as there is alignment between what I am doing and who I am.

Once you choose to no longer blame anyone, including yourself, you begin to see opportunities you never saw before, and you perceive your past in a more positive light, knowing it has shaped you in a way nothing else could as powerfully, and you relinquish the urge for control as a self-defence mechanism and the world opens up to you. There is nothing to worry about. You have been tricked and compelled towards pessimism as a default way of being, when in fact you are so much more glorious and powerful than what you perceive yourself to be.

Once you get a taste of your self-directing powers, you cannot go back to allowing events of society to dictate your own moves, and you also stop blaming society because you know that collective consciousness shapes reality and there is nothing unfair about it, and in the silence you can hear it, a low hum, the peace of total consciousness within, a reminder of our divinity, and something is shifting in this world, people are beginning to feel it, a soulful experiential awakening, a growing awareness that the external script they were handed was never written for their liberation. True societal change happens at the scale of mass individual change. Anything else occurs through force via external authority. This is why I write. Not to preach. But because the individual who stops blaming and starts directing is the only unit of change that has ever mattered.

Contentment is not happiness. It is not the absence of sadness. It is the perpetual realisation that one is connected to something divine, while the soul moves through the human. That is what becomes available when you stop blaming. Not perfection. Not arrival. Just that.

I cannot see how life can go wrong if I just follow my heart truthfully.
