I feel like I am always rushing and never arriving anywhere.

Reading the essays…

soulsensis AI

You are not the only one, and the rush itself may be the thing to question. In “Where are you rushing to?” the writer puts it plainly: “Everyone feels rushed, but no one is asking: where are you rushing to?” Going faster does not fix the feeling of never arriving. “Acceleration only gets you to a place faster. It does not mean you are happier. It just means you will have another goal sooner.”

So try the small thing the essay suggests. “Tomorrow morning, before you pick up your phone, before the first goal of the day, ask yourself one thing: where am I rushing to?” If you cannot answer it, that is your sign to slow down until you can.

Why do I never get what I want?

Reading the essays…

soulsensis AI

“You get who you are” turns your question around: “you get who you are, not what you want. We never get what we want. Because wanting implies lack of having.” So the shift it suggests is to start with your state, not the thing: “If you had the money, how would you feel? Relaxed and more giving? Certainly.” Begin feeling and acting that way now.

“You do not get what you want” adds why waiting for someone to fix it does not work: “growth is in the process and cannot be offloaded to an external entity.” Ask yourself who you would be if you already had what you want, and be that person in one small way today.
