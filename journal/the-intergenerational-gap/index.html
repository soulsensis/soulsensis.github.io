The intergenerational gap

22 December 2019

Note that this piece of writing isn’t perfected (I mean, when has my writing ever been?) and it is on a topic I’ll certainly revisit in the future. Firstly, I probably should clarify that I refer to the inherent differences between myself and my parents as an intergenerational gap. Pretty sure we’re all on the same page about this so imma just dive into it.

Firstly, I want to make a disclaimer that I love my parents (no shit) but in order to analyse effectively, one must also be as critical as possible. God damn why is this SO damn hard to WRITE MAN.

Ok so let’s just pick one topic and roll with it.

Communication

This is a tough one. I speak Vietnamese with my parents at home so this creates an unusual dynamic for myself. Firstly, I’m fucking glad I am able to speak viet fluently – it’s not as fluent as I’d like it to be but it gets me by, I studied it to VCE (year 12) so I can’t be too horrendous right? LOL. Ok not gonna lie I’m actually pre fucking good but compared to my parent's standard, I'm shit all.

This makes it SO DAMN DIFFICULT whenever I want to express my point of view. For those that may not be aware (i.e. non-asians reading this haha), Asian parents by default have a superiority complex whereby they posit themselves as being always correct. It is considered an unusually rare sighting (lol) to see an Asian parent retract any statements they make. I don’t believe this is even ego-related, it’s just tradition. You’re never meant to argue with authority. My parents believe they’re right, perhaps just as much as I believe I’m right, which makes our dynamic, at times, uncomfortable and tense...
