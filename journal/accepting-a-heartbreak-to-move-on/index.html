Accepting a heartbreak to move on

19 July 2020

Before we met, I always had the assumption that life was tragic, difficult and hard. The road to happiness seemed arduous, perhaps even a little too much to bear at times as I'd question so many things wrong with life and the nature of existence.

Everything changed when you came along. You're the warm summer breeze capable of uplifting the most downtrodden soul with your radiating smile. We kicked things off so quickly and through this haste, it seemed like all my life dissatisfaction disappeared overnight.

We fell in love quickly. We fell in love hard. It was all a new experience for the both of us as we fell for each other deeply and so profoundly. The way you'd hold my arm as we walked around uni campus and the quick peck on the lips when we had to go to our separate classes.

One of the most tragic elements of going through those fiery, honeymoon stages of a relationship, is the inability to realise that you're going through, perhaps, the best days of the relationship. The days where you don't have any care in the world. The days where all you can think about was when is the next time you can see this other person.

Our lives became so entwined as our friendship groups merged, and people loved seeing us together because we exemplified what it was like to be in a truly happy relationship. A relationship so grounded in love and altruism that people simply could not ignore - people were rooting for us to work out, to demonstrate that true love exists.

Now that we're separated, there's nothing but the memories to validate the fact that we were once in love - so in love with each and every aspect of each other. But as I sit here reminiscing it's so easy to lose track of reality, which is the fact that we're done, it's over and we must move on for the sake of our love.

Whether these memories are forgotten or not, whether it's you or me who forgets them, does it really matter? The fact that we were once so madly in love will always be an immutable truth where its existence transcends any form of memory. Simply put, we don't need to hold onto them anymore. Specifically, I need to let you go and these memories go…

The more I hold onto you, the more I want to relive these memories, the more I'm preventing myself from enjoying my present life with the people around me. My life is so different without you and perhaps that's okay. It's as what they say, out with the old - in with the new.

It's time to embrace what this new chapter of my life has to offer. The fact that we were once so in love with each other is a beautiful thing and shall remain a beautiful thing whether or not I hold onto these memories of us. It's so easy to glorify a past where you know you were once so happy in each other's presence, but if I keep doing this, I'm just going to keep breaking down every time I think of you.

I'm learning, step by step, to translate these emotions of longing and sadness into one of acceptance and appreciation for what we've had. This isn't easy, it hurts me to my core to try and let you go but the key motivation for me to do so is that I must continue to live my life. I cannot keep holding on, it hurts too much to keep thinking about those nights where we keep talking until morning on the phone and falling asleep, missing class just to be with each other a bit longer, the natural obsession of a first love as we snuck out at night just to make out in the back-seat.

I'm sitting here, left with all these memories and I wonder how you're dealing with them. You seemed to have moved on quite well. A part of me hurts to see you doing well because it solidifies my realisation that I'm struggling so damn hard compared to you, yet a part of me is so proud of you for moving on and embracing all that your life has to offer because the truth is, you deserve the very best and I know good things are coming to you.

The only difficulty now is for me to realise that perhaps, just maybe, some good things are coming my way too. It's just so hard to see this as a possibility especially when I'm still holding on too hard to our past. A past that is long gone now, replaced by a bitter heartache that I haven't a clue when it'll subside.

I only have faith that one day, perhaps we'll meet again as different people. Whether something can ignite again between us isn't even up for consideration, it's more just to see how we're doing, how our past love has changed us, hopefully, for the better. I want to hear about your professional and personal accolades, I want to know how your family is doing, the family that I thought I'd one day completely assimilate to. Either way, I believe that we'll move forward from this with futureproofed optimism for the sake of our past love.

I will always miss you, and that's okay. It means that what we've had was special, and I'm content with such a realisation.
