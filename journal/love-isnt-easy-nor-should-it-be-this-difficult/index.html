Love isn't easy, nor should it be this difficult

5 April 2020

I wept my tears away, struggling to comprehend what was wrong with me. Why have I concluded that whether I stay or leave, it’ll hurt either way? What kind of sickening emotion is this? Is this paralysis? But isn’t love meant to be difficult? Shouldn’t we hold on and try to make the impossible work? To prove to ourselves that our love can ascend beyond these menial disparages?

Love isn’t easy but nor should it be difficult. The difficulty should rest in achieving a balance, to walk the line between yin & yang. A very dear friend of mine introduced to me the idea of “you do you”. Although simple, to me that is eye-opening and is perhaps one of the reliefs I’ve needed to recover from heartache. My ex and I both felt we couldn’t be ourselves anymore. How terrible is that? Imagine if love involved a compromise, not on family, not on other extraneous elements, but rather, a compromise on the freedom to exhibit SELF. I believe that isn’t healthy but rather dangerous as we lose individuality for the sake of love.

I am fortunate to have broken up healthily. There is no blame. No ill will, anger, toxicity, revenge. Only tears… and that’s okay. It’s okay.

It’s been over two months now and I’ve perhaps learned more about myself in these two months than the 4.5 years of my relationship.

Why, you ask? It’s because I was comfortable. Too comfortable, perhaps. If you’re like me and indulge in a relationship as a crutch, offloading the burdens of life that you should be addressing or the lessons that life wants to teach you. You’re not in love, you’re just ignorant. It also isn’t fair on your partner. It’s not fair at all. I mean, she needs to grow as well, she has her own life to live.

I’m quite self-critical, but I have to be. To make sense of your past you must learn to delve into it, without judgement, and face the reality of what you’ve done. That’s the only way you can grow, in my opinion. Yes, sure, you should move on and all. But the question is… HOW can one move on? Everyone’s different. For me though, moving on involves a deep assessment of your past, discovering various quirks and idiosyncrasies which perhaps dictated your behaviour, thought patterns and overall functionality.

I’m no saint, but god damn it, I wish I was. I’ve made mistakes just like everyone else. But what I hope to differ is in my ability to look at my mistake, eye to eye, to confront it properly, without a semblance of an excuse, without shame, and give it the required acknowledgement and attention it deserves. After doing so can I only say it’s time to “move on”.

As much as I miss her, I also know that perhaps 80% of it has now eventuated to missing the idea of her. To have the comradery, that life companion as a form of future security is uplifting and arguably mandatory during a time of unprecedented uncertainty. But maybe, it isn’t as mandatory as I think…

As I recover, I learn that the ultimate form of security is the ability for me to be comfortable in my own skin. If I’m unable to be comfortable alone, how am I to impart comfort to other people in a way that’s healthy? I’ll end up suffering another form of anxiety which involves compulsively seeking human interaction to avoid having to face loneliness. I’ve vowed to work on the ability to be comfortable, not in the presence of other people — because that’s too easy — but rather, in the presence of no one else but myself, my mind, my racing thoughts, my anxiety-prone mentality, and my fragile as fuck heart.

Love isn’t easy guys, but again, nor should it be difficult. Perhaps the toughest form of love isn’t the ability to love someone else, but rather, the ability to love yourself. And boy, that’s quite a journey to undertake.

But it’s okay, I’m loving the journey thus far.

Take care of yourselves guys, and thanks for reading.

What are we but a conglomerate of atoms which, for some miracle, able to possess conscious (and subconscious) thought? Atoms which one day will return to where it came from. But our thoughts? No — those can perpetuate for eternity, depending on how strongly they resonate to other conglomerates of atoms (which for some miracle also possess the ability to think…).
