I don't think this is too much to ask for

28 April 2020

I've been telling myself that I'm doing okay. I'm not ready to date again and to go through all the volatility and emotions of a relationship. It's not worth it, right? - so I tell myself.

As I am walking the streets of this brisk winter evening, I couldn't help but hear the faint sounds of pots and pans, the hustling and chatter of families as they arrange the dinner table, and the sound of children enjoying the received attention as they cause a ruckus at home.

It'd be nice I thought, to come home after a long day of work... to this, something so simple, yet so beautiful. To come home to a family that exists because of you, to be greeted by the chaos and mess that are your children, to come home to a loving husband who hugs and warms your dreary soul – a soul worn out from the hours of corporate life.

My heart sank as I thought of my ex, someone who I had misplaced all these expectations on. Someone who I thought would one day be the father of my children – children who now shall never be born. The pain of it made me flinch. As I wiped away tears I felt as though I was wiping away all the pain of heartbreak. It's been months but sometimes it still feels like yesterday when we were skipping class to go on a road trip, with only the sound of music and laughter to guide us, to guide a love that was to last another 4 years.

I do miss him. I miss his smile. I miss how he'd always rest his hand gently on the small of my back everywhere we go. I miss how he'd be the life of a party, striking up conversations with everyone, attempting to spread positivity to all he met. He always told me "life's short, you got to make the most of it". I never understood him at that depth. Why he'd go so out of his way to make friends with those who didn't appreciate him – how he'd love them despite living with some deeply rooted insecurities.

I love how every time I reminisce about him it's always the positive memories. Yet, when I was with him, I could only focus on what was holding us back from my ideal relationship. When we decided to call it off, it was the realisation that we were too different, polarised at our core.

I clutched my shawl tighter and capitulated my face into my scarf as a wind blew aggressively, slicing through my reverie. And just like that, I was again, back to reality, a reality where my dreams remain as dreams and my hopes for a better future remain docile in the face of life's complexities.

I don't think what I yearn for is a lot to ask. I see my husband next to me, eyes brimming with love as he's humming away to some song that's completely lost in translation. He was marinating some ribs as I was preparing vegetables, our arms lightly touching, a warm and gentle reminder of our love.

It was a beautiful scene, but what stood out to me was its simplicity. Love shouldn't need to be something extravagant. It shouldn't need to be living in mansion-sized residences with butlers. It shouldn't need to be fine dining at socially approved, sensationalised restaurants. It shouldn't need to take convincing each other of your love as you're buying the fourth handbag for her for the month.

Love can be easy, it can be modest – only if you allow it to be. And right now, it's just me and the love of my life preparing dinner under the roof of our suburban home and for once…

… I don't think this is too much to ask for.
