I never thought I'd end up with someone like you

16 May 2020

She was on her phone, snapchatting the coffee I just bought her.

"Why do people have to snapchat fucking everything these days," I thought, thankfully not out loud though. It was always a pet peeve of mine, watching people who just seems so lost in the moment - and not necessarily in a good way. They're just lost in the moment, having deprived themselves of a true and genuine experience of drinking a good coffee. Living life through a fucking phone lens. What a joke.

I always knew my cynicism was bad, but this was just maddening… "Why are you looking so grumpy for?" she inquired, eyes genuinely brimming with curiosity. Ah shit should I just tell her the truth? My thoughts on society and all this stupid social media consumption that's just fucking making every bitter and resentful. I don't know - I kinda sound bitter and resentful right now about this whole ordeal. It's probably best to play it cool, no need for truth-bombs right now.

"Just thinking about some trouble at home, mum and dad aren't getting along well lately," I could not believe the words that just came out. So it seemed I decided to not tell her the truth and I told her another, perhaps, even more personal truth? The fuck's wrong with me?

Her tone shifted to concern "hmmm… I'm sorry to hear… This stuff isn't easy hey, are you going alright though? I'm definitely keen to hear more if you want to share, no pressure." I find myself doing this too much - intentionally cutting small talk to talk about more meaningful things. I mean, I'm not lying - it obviously has been on my mind. Why the fuck else would I bring it up on a casual coffee date?

"Nah it's fine," gotta play it off cool, I thought, "it's beyond my control, can't really do much but just keep positive."

"That's true," she said, as she flipped her phone screen face down and leaned in. She was a lot more beautiful than I thought, maybe her low cut top made me a tad biased. "I don't know, sometimes I feel like I should speak up more at home you know? Make my parents realise that what they're doing actually impacts on the mental health of my siblings and myself. It's just frustrating when parents argue openly, it's a shit example to set for kids."

Woah what the hell is happening? I'm feeling myself getting pulled in to this conversation a lot faster than I anticipated. I mean, I kinda asked for it - no small talk just raw talk.

"I'm not gonna lie, I've actually thought about what you've said very deeply at one point - especially the thought of saying something and having an input but it's just so damn hard to do it. As their kid, there seems to be an underlying obligation to just listen to them, do what you're told, and leave these issues to the 'adults'…" I paused for a moment, noticing that she was still leaning in, engaged, thoughtful, and annoyingly beautiful, "You're right though, mental health is so important. Like why am I even telling you about this right now? It's just so out of the blue but clearly it's been on my mind a lot lately to the point where I inadvertently just blurt it out."

She nodded, slowly and surely. Her pensiveness was very attractive to me. I like it when people take their time to think before they talk. It's always good to pause and think first before speaking, you come off 10 times more sophisticated, because you are, and secondly, whatever you say indubitably makes more sense.

"I'm surprised you brought it up too, normally people don't like to engage in these kind of conversation but these personal struggles are like bread and butter to me." She was soft-spoken yet very deliberate and careful in her inflection as she continued, "I enjoy these types of conversations because it tells you about the other person so much more than talking about the damn weather. I mean, it's completely normal and all and I don't want to belittle those who indulge in small talk because at times, it's quite necessary. It just isn't for me."

Yeah, this is fucked.

Honestly, right now, she could be a 4/10 looks and I'd ask her out for a second date. As our conversation went on, we kept connecting at this deep humanistic level which seemed to transcend so many socially stigmatised boundaries. The conversation was like an artform to me, I just hope she feels the same.

…

10 dates later, she's now sitting next to me and we're going on a road trip to Sydney, stopping by along the NSW coast along the way.

"Babe can you please for the love of god change the music," she pointed at the radio.

"This is my bluetooth, what the fuck that's mean as," I retorted

"That's my point, I'd rather the radio than to listen to this stupid K-Pop. You don't even understand it, I've yet to see you download a language app or study Korean even though at one point you were trying to impress me that you knew "a bit of it"."

Damn she's savage, alright I'll pull out my big guns, "sweetie, you do know that the beauty of music comes from feeling it, not necessarily understanding it. You can clearly tell this girl is singing her heart over some long term relo. It's expressive and beautiful."

"If you don't change it now, you'll have no chance for a long term relo with me, maybe then you'll start singing korean."

I grinned, shaking my head and obliged, changing it to radio.

My heart's never felt so full.
