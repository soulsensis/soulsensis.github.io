I woke up to her crying

17 June 2020

It wasn't uncontrolled. You can tell she was trying to keep herself together. Once you've lived with someone most of your life, you have a sense that something was wrong. Her crying was quiet but was not undetectable over the gentle hum of kitchen appliances outside our room as she leaned into me, quivering with tears.

She's been like this for a while and I'm struggling to understand what exactly is going on. Is it me? It is her work? Is it her depression acting up? All I could do was hold her, as I felt the warmth of her body against mine whilst reassuring her, "everything's going to be ok, sweetie."

"How do you know that? How could you possibly understand what I've been going through? All you do is give me solutions, you never listen to me - truly, listen to me. I just want you to listen for a change." Her words we muffled as she was still against me. Although it hurt to hear them, that's the thing about love, you're able to say things to each other without the sugar coating. We always get to the truth, as harsh as it may sound.

I was silent, stroking her hair, and most importantly, listening. She was right, I've always tried to offer solutions to her myriad of problems. But lately, I've come to realise that perhaps the real solution for some scenarios is to just listen and not to offer any advice. When you offer advice, no matter how good-willed you are, it may at times be misconstrued as a lack of empathy. Perhaps right now, I'm dealing with the years of solution-finding as she's still crying in my arms.

"I know you love me, and I know you're hurting from my accusations right now and I’m so sorry to put you through this." she pulled back and looked up into my eyes. We were still in bed, our bodies in a tangled mess. Everything seemed so normal, so perfect, yet this sobering reality of a conversation held a strange aura over us, rendering me a little anxious. Something didn't seem right.

"I'm not happy, I haven't been happy in years."

No amount of love could cushion the blow of hearing this from your life partner. I grimaced, clearly shaken by her words. "What do you mean?" I questioned, although not genuinely wanting to hear the ensuing reasons for fear of how much it'll hurt. It's as though a deep chasm has opened up between us and our hearts now felt a million miles away from each other as she composed herself. No longer crying.

"I haven't been happy in a long time, I just haven't been able to tell you. Objectively, we look so perfect. Our friends love us, we're the ideal for so many people that we know. My girlfriends love hearing about the things you do for me and as you've told me, you speak so highly of me to all your friends too. I know we're perfect for each other. I know things have been going so well. In light of all this, I'm just not happy and I've been feeling like this for years now. What's wrong with me?"

The truth is, I didn't know what was wrong. Maybe I have been a bit emotionally distant. She told me she understood why I've been like this but no excuse can justify why I've made her unhappy for years. All this seemed too much for me as I listened on, careful to really listen to her and for once, not offer any solutions. Perhaps there wasn't a solution to where we are right now. I felt myself grow distant from her with every word she said, with every accusation mentioned, and whilst I grew antagonised, wanting to defend myself, for some strange reason, I sensed a deep sense of futility as she seemed to have already made up her mind on this matter.

"What do we do now?" I asked.

None of us said a word for a whole minute as we just looked at each other. Words didn't seem necessary. Something very fundamental has shifted within us. Her eyes no longer resemble the girl I fell in love with 20 years ago. Her presence was no longer one of warmth but of icy chills as I anticipated for what is going to happen to us now that I've seemingly lost her…

"I cry most nights you know, but for some reason you seemed to notice it today."
